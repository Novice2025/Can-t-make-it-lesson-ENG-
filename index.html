Brazilian Business Communication

Learn to communicate like a native.

The method is the **"Lego-block"** approach, where each phrase and expression is a block that fits into another, creating a professional and seamless structure.

The First Block: The Soft Touch

Always start with a polite introduction. The phrase **"I am afraid..."** (Infelizmente...) is the opening block.

I am afraid...

This phrase doesn't mean "I'm scared"! It's a very polite way to deliver bad news, like "I'm sorry, but..." or "Unfortunately...". Using it shows respect and professionalism.

The Second Block: The Empathetic Refusal

The next step is to be direct and clear about what you can't do. The phrase **"...I can't make it"** (Não vou conseguir) is perfect for this.

I can't make it.

This is the professional way to say "I can't go," "I can't attend," or "I won't be able to." It is much more polite than just "I can't go." or "I'm busy."

The Third Block: The Professional Justification

Natives appreciate it when you give a brief and clear reason. The phrases **"I have a prior commitment"** (Tenho um compromisso pré-existente) or **"I am double-booked"** (Tenho dois compromissos no mesmo horário) are ideal.

I have a prior commitment. / I am double-booked.

The first means you already have a scheduled engagement. The second, slightly more informal but still professional, means your calendar has two events at the same time. Both are better than the vague "I'm busy."

The Fourth Block: The Unforeseen Event

For those last-minute moments, use these phrases as justification. The main concept to remember is **(Something just came up)** (Algo de última hora surgiu).

  • 👉 Something urgent just popped up.
  • 👉 Something unexpected just came up.
  • � A critical issue just arose.

All these phrases mean that something unexpected and important has happened, and you need to deal with it immediately. It's a professional and polite way to communicate an unforeseen event.

The Fifth Block: The Solution

Don't just decline, offer an alternative. The solution can be one of two forms:

👉 "May I take a rain check?" (Podemos deixar para outro dia?)

This phrase, which literally means "can I get a ticket for a rainy day?", is a very common idiom to propose postponing an engagement to an undefined future date.

👉 "Would you mind if we reschedule?" (Você se importaria se remarcássemos?)

This is a direct and polite question that immediately suggests rescheduling the commitment for a new day and time, showing that you are still committed.

The Sixth Block: The Empathy

Adding a sincere apology shows that you care about the other person's schedule. Use **"I am sorry."** (Sinto muito).

I am sorry.

Although "I am afraid" already has a touch of apology, adding "I am sorry" to the beginning of the complete sentence demonstrates even more politeness and empathy, which is highly valued in the American workplace.

The Seventh Block: The Rescheduling Proposal

You can use questions to make proposals in a more informal and friendly, but still professional, way.

👉 "How about...?" (Que tal...?)

This expression is ideal for suggesting a date or time in a friendly manner. For example: "**How about** Thursday at 3 PM?".

👉 "How's that for...?" (O que acha de...?)

A great way to offer an alternative and ask for the other person's opinion. Example: "I can do next Tuesday, **how's that for** a new time?".

👉 "Would tomorrow work for you?" (Amanhã daria certo para você?)

Another direct and polite alternative for checking someone's availability.

The Eighth Block: The Confirmation

Unlike "I can't make it," these phrases are for confirming your attendance in a clear and positive way.

👉 "I can make it." (Eu consigo ir)

This is the direct and most common confirmation, used to say "I can go" or "I will be able to."

👉 "I'll be there." (Estarei lá)

A little more casual and emphatic, it means "I will be there" and conveys a strong intention to attend.

👉 "Looking forward to it!" (Estou ansioso por isso!)

This phrase not only confirms your presence but also expresses enthusiasm. It's ideal for social events, happy hours, or any enjoyable gathering.

👉 "See you there!" (A gente se vê lá!)

A casual and friendly phrase used to confirm that you will be present and are excited about the idea. It's a common goodbye for informal get-togethers.

Situations at Work

Here are some examples of how to combine the blocks in common day-to-day office situations.

Important Note:

The key to effective communication is not a literal translation. Notice how the Portuguese and English phrases, despite having different structures, convey the same intention. Instead of translating word for word, focus on learning the language "block" that expresses the desired idea in a natural and professional way.

Declining a last-minute commitment:

Shoot, I have to cancel lunch today. I'm sorry, but something urgent just came up with my project. I can't make it.
👉 "I'm sorry, I'm afraid I can't make it. Something urgent just came up. Would you mind if we reschedule for tomorrow?"

Dude, I won't be able to make it to the alignment meeting today. We can talk tomorrow, right? Please?
👉 "I'm afraid I can't make it to the alignment meeting today. How about we catch up on the project tomorrow?"

Declining and proposing a new date:

I won't be able to attend the agenda meeting right now. My calendar is packed. How about we talk tomorrow?
👉 "I have a prior commitment, so I'm afraid I can't make it. How about we meet tomorrow instead?"

I can't come to your desk to review the document today. I'm in the middle of a super important call. Can we do this later, like, after 3 PM?
👉 "I'm afraid I can't come to your desk to review the document right now. I'm in the middle of a super important call. Would you mind if we reschedule for later, how about after 3 PM?"

Confirming attendance at an event:

What great news! I can attend the event. I'll be there to support the team and meet everyone.
👉 "I can make it! I'll be there to support the team. I'm really looking forward to it!"

Hey everyone, great! I got the happy hour invitation. I'm definitely going! Let's do it!
👉 "Hey team, thanks for the invite to the happy hour! I can make it. Looking forward to it!"

Asking for help with a task:

Hey, what's up? Can you give me a hand with this presentation? I'm swamped.
👉 "Hey, would you mind lending me a hand with this presentation? I'm swamped."

Following up on an email:

Hi, how are you? Just wanted to know if you managed to see my message. Can we talk about the project status?
👉 "Hi, I hope you're doing well. Just wanted to follow up on my last email. Can we connect to discuss the project status?"
